# Integration of functional immunomonitoring assays with PET/CT scans in TB patients identifies on-treatment biomarkers
# SUMMARY
Tuberculosis (TB) continues to pose a significant global public health challenge with substantial patient morbidity and mortality. Current TB patient biomarkers lack sufficient resolution to inform different treatment responses. This necessitates the development of sensitive and reliable host biomarkers. We previously demonstrated the efficacy of TruCulture whole blood stimulation for differentiating asymptomatic TB from active pulmonary TB patients in endemic regions. This systems immunology study expands upon our previous work by evaluating the potential of TruCulture to monitor longitudinal responses to TB treatment in patients from the Predict-TB trial before, during, and after antibiotic therapy. We stimulated whole blood from TB patients (n=40) using TruCulture under four conditions (Null, Mycobacterium tuberculosis-antigen, LPS, and IL-1β) at baseline (week 0), following treatment (weeks 16 and 24), and one-year post-treatment (week 72). 20/25 detectable cytokines exhibited significant changes throughout treatment, with several continuing to evolve during post-therapy follow-up. Machine learning based analysis identified Mtb-Ag-induced IL-1RA (AUC = 0.90, 0.92, 0.95 at weeks 16, 24, 72) and LPS-induced NLRP3 (AUC = 0.94 at week 16) as the best protein and transcriptional biomarkers for distinguishing pre-treatment from on-treatment and post-treatment time points, strongly implicating the inflammasome response. Combining these results with the extent of lung disease assessed by FDG PET/CT scans, we showed direct disease relevance for these blood-based biomarkers. The identified biomarker profiles hold promise for improving TB patient care through early prediction of treatment response, real-time therapy monitoring, and informed development of host-directed therapeutic strategies for clinical decision-making.
# Repository organization
The repository is structured as a fully reproducible R Project using Quarto (`.qmd`) computational pipelines. Pre-rendered `.html` reports are included so you can browse the exact figures, statistical outputs, and code execution.
